The idea behind the title

Pink lemons are real.

There is a variety of Eureka lemon that grows with green-and-cream stripes on the outside and pale pink flesh on the inside. As it ripens, the green fades and the rind starts to look like the yellow lemon most of us expect. Hand somebody one without explaining, and they would say, “That’s a lemon.” They would be right. They just wouldn’t know everything about what they were holding.

Chris Marvel Davis spent years doing the same thing with losing. Something happened, he named it a loss, and the explanation showed up right behind it: work harder, want it more, prove them wrong. Sometimes that advice was exactly right. A lot of the time it was answering a different problem than the one in front of him.

The Pink Lemon is about what happens before the lemonade. You look at what you actually have, you cut it open, and you check whether the inside matches the story you already told yourself about the outside.

“The event happens first. Then we start deciding what the event says about us. Those two things can get so close together that they feel like one thought.”

The framework

What kind of L was it?

The book sorts losses into five broad kinds, which Chris calls The Five. He is clear that it isn’t science. It’s a way to slow down long enough to ask what actually happened before reaching for the advice you already believe. Tap a segment.

    About the author

    Chris Marvel Davis

    Chris Marvel Davis is a speaker, trainer and the author of The What If Effect. He holds a bachelor’s and a master’s degree in psychology and has spent more than ten years in front of rooms full of students, athletes, educators and leaders, talking about performance, pressure and what people do after things go wrong.

    He grew up in Cleveland on Indians baseball and library weekends with his dad, and now lives in the Atlanta area with his wife, Jazmine, and their three sons. The Pink Lemon came out of a stretch where he had to look harder at his own losses in sports, business and family life, and stop answering every one of them the same way.
